Factorising: brackets backwards 🔄

Hi Maya! 👋 You can turn 3(x + 4) into 3x + 12. Today you'll go the other way and put the brackets back in. About 20 minutes.

🎬 🃏 🧲 🕵️ ✏️ 🧠
🎬

Watch first

5 min

Tap ▶ to play. Listen out for the words "highest common factor".

The end of the video takes out letters too, like x(x + 3). That's for another day, so stop when it gets there if you like.

🃏

Deal the tiles

4 min
⭐ The big idea Factorising is expanding backwards. 3(x + 4) was 3 equal rows of tiles. So to factorise, share the tiles into equal rows.

How to play: tap a button to deal the tiles into that many rows, like dealing cards. 🟢 Green rows are all the same. 🔴 Red rows don't match. Find the most rows that work.

🧲

Pull out the number

4 min
📖 New words A common factor goes into both numbers. The highest common factor (HCF) is the biggest one. For 6 and 15 it's 3. Put the HCF outside the bracket, then divide to see what's left inside.

How to play: drag the right pink number into the pink box outside the bracket (or tap it). Then pick what goes inside. Get through 5 for 5 stars ⭐

🕵️

Spot the mistake

3 min

Some of these are right, some are wrong, and some are right but not fully factorised: the numbers inside could still share something.

🔍 Detective trick Expand it back! If 3(2x + 5) expands to the question, it's right.

Then look inside the bracket: do 2 and 5 share a factor? No? Then it's fully factorised ✓

How to play: drag each card into the right box (or tap a card, then tap a box).

✏️

Your turn

5 min

Factorise fully. Type it with brackets, like 3(2x + 5). Press Enter or Check.

Get 8 right to finish. Some have a minus, like Lesson 2. If you slip, you'll see why.

🧠
