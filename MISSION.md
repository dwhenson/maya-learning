# Mission: Expanding brackets (KS3, Year 8)

## Why
Maya is in Year 8 and expanding brackets has been taught in class but hasn't clicked yet: single brackets like 3(x + 4) still cause mistakes, especially with negatives. The goal is a strong mental model of *why* expanding works, so the method stops feeling like a trick to memorise and she can do it confidently in lessons.

## Success looks like
- Can explain, with a picture (the area model), why 3(x + 4) = 3x + 12
- Expands single brackets correctly every time, including negative multipliers like −2(x − 5)
- Can expand and simplify expressions like 3(x + 2) + 2(x − 1)
- Can spot and fix the classic mistakes (only multiplying the first term; sign errors)
- (Later) Expands double brackets like (x + 2)(x + 3) using the same model

## Constraints
- Maya works through lessons on her own; Claude is the teacher for follow-up questions
- No deadline: depth of understanding over speed
- Short lessons with practice and instant feedback

## Out of scope
- Factorising (the reverse) until expanding is solid
- GCSE-level content (e.g. expanding three brackets, surds)
